# How to Write Government Notings Under CSMOP Office Procedure

A government noting constitutes the official examination of an administrative matter on the notes portion of a physical or digital file. It provides the competent authority with the background, rules, precedents, and options necessary to make an informed, lawful decision. Beyond serving immediate administrative needs, a note establishes the permanent public record of executive rationale.

The Central Secretariat Manual of Office Procedure (CSMOP), published by the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG), lays down the mandatory standards for processing receipts, referencing documents, consulting divisions, and framing recommendations across ministries and departments.

---

## The Meaning and Purpose of a Noting

A noting is not a mechanical summary of documents placed on a file, nor is it personal correspondence between officers. It is a formal administrative assessment.

Every complete noting answers five essential questions:

* What specific issue requires determination?
* What are the established, material facts of the case?
* Which statutory provisions, rules, or executive instructions govern the matter?
* What operational, legal, or financial options exist?
* What precise decision or sanction is sought from the competent authority?

The note must be accurate, traceable, and legally defensible. A decision-maker must be able to rely upon the integrity of the statements made on the note sheet without independently verifying basic administrative data.

---

## Fundamental Principles of Note Drafting

Adherence to core drafting conventions prevents file bloat and bureaucratic delays.

### Conciseness Without Omission

Notes must remain disciplined and focused. Detailed calculations, technical specifications, and historical correspondence should be relegated to an Appendix to Notes or flagged enclosures. Conciseness never excuses the omission of material facts; omitting a known legal risk or financial liability produces a defective note that exposes the administration to legal challenge or audit strictures.

### The Functional Approach

CSMOP prescribes that the volume and detail of a note must align with the nature of the matter:

| Category of Case | Prescribed Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| **Ephemeral or routine matters** | Minimal endorsement or direct disposal without formal noting. |
| **Simple correspondence** | Brief factual note or immediate action on file. |
| **Repetitive transactions** | Standard Process Sheet (SPS) with standard checks. |
| **Problem-solving matters** | Structured note analyzing facts, legal grounds, and comparative options. |
| **Policy and planning files** | Comprehensive note examining financial liabilities, socio-economic impacts, and statutory implications. |

### Consecutive Numbered Paragraphs

Noting must be arranged in numbered paragraphs (1, 2, 3) to allow precise cross-referencing during discussions and in subsequent orders. Distinct subsidiary points under a main heading should use decimal numbering (e.g., 2.1, 2.2) or alphabetic markers. Bulleted lists are discouraged in core running notings where office manuals mandate paragraph-based citation.

## Preliminary Scrutiny Before Recording a Note

Before entering a single line on the green note sheet or within an e-office file module, the dealing hand or Section Officer must complete four verification steps:

* **Receipt Classification:** Confirm whether the communication is a Paper Under Consideration (PUC) requiring disposal, or a Fresh Receipt (FR) bringing supplemental information to an existing case. Label multiple fresh receipts sequentially (FR-I, FR-II).
* **File Completeness:** Ensure that all referenced enclosures, linked orders, previous notes, and required financial estimates are placed in the correspondence portion and appropriately flagged.
* **File Selection:** Place receipts on existing live files dealing with the subject matter. Opening fresh files for interconnected matters violates records management protocols and fragments administrative memory.
* **Urgency Scrutiny:** Scrutinize priority markings (Immediate, Priority, Court Case, Parliament Question, VIP Reference). Urgency indicators must match administrative reality rather than serving as routine handling labels.

## Structural Architecture of a Complete Note

A standard noting must adhere to a standardized, logical sequence to simplify executive review.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  File Number & Crisp Subject Line                      │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Para 1: The Core Issue & PUC Citation                 │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Paras 2–3: Material Chronology & Verified Facts       │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Para 4: Statutory Provisions, GFR, Rules & Precedents │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Paras 5–6: Administrative Analysis & IFD Concurrence  │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Para 7: Concrete Recommendation / Approval Footing    │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Sign-off Block (Name, Designation, Date, Intercom)    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

```

### 1. File Number and Subject Line

The header must clearly identify the file identity and a specific, unambiguous subject:

```
File No.: A-12011/03/2026-Admn
Subject: Procurement of 25 Desktop Computers for the Regional Office—Reg.

```

Avoid open-ended labels such as "Purchase matter" or "Regarding sanction of funds."

### 2. Identifying the Issue Under Consideration

The opening paragraph must pinpoint the incoming communication and summarize the relief, action, or sanction demanded:

```
1. The PUC at Flag 'A', pp. 5–8/c, relates to a proposal received from the 
Regional Office seeking administrative approval and expenditure sanction for the 
procurement of 25 desktop computers to replace obsolete computing equipment.

```

### 3. Chronology and Background

Provide a selective administrative history. Avoid documenting routine internal file movements. Focus entirely on prior approvals, baseline inventory data, and current operational constraints:

```
2. The Regional Office was allotted 40 desktop computers during the financial 
year 2019–20. As per the technical assessment report, 25 of these units have 
completed their prescribed useful economic life of five years and are incapable 
of supporting current enterprise database applications.

3. The requirement has been certified by the Head of Office vide communication 
dated 12.09.2026, and the requisite technical specifications conform to the 
standard benchmarks issued by MeitY (Flag 'B', p. 12/c).

```

### 4. Statutory Provisions and Rules

Cite the governing rules with exact clauses and dates. Never use vague terms such as "as per government rules":

```
4. Rule 149 of the General Financial Rules (GFR), 2017 mandates the procurement 
of common-use goods through the Government e-Marketplace (GeM). The proposal 
satisfies the prerequisites prescribed under the Departmental Procurement 
Manual read with Ministry of Finance OM No. F.1/26/2025-PPD dated 15.01.2025.

```

### 5. Examination, Inter-Division Consultation, and Financial Liabilities

Evaluate the operational merits against administrative authority, budget allocations, and necessary statutory concurrences:

```
5. The proposal involves a one-time non-recurring expenditure of \$22,500 
(₹18,75,000), inclusive of applicable GST. Budget provision exists under Major 
Head "2052", Minor Head "00.090", Object Head "Office Expenses" for FY 2026–27. 
An estimated annual maintenance liability of ₹1,20,000 will arise from the 
third year onward.

6. The proposal was referred to the Internal Finance Division (IFD) for expenditure 
concurrence. IFD has examined the proposal and concurred vide Note No. 12 on 
page 4/n, subject to procurement through the GeM portal and observance of delegated 
powers under the Delegation of Financial Powers Rules (DFPR).

```

### 6. Actionable Recommendation and Submission Footing

Draft the closing paragraphs so the competent authority can grant approval without adding conditional language:

```
7. In view of the position stated above, approval of the competent authority 
is solicited for:

7.1 Administrative approval and expenditure sanction for ₹18,75,000 (Rupees 
Eighteen Lakh Seventy-Five Thousand only) toward the procurement of 25 desktop 
computers via the GeM portal; and

7.2 Authorizing the Head of Office to execute the procurement process in terms 
of Rule 149 of GFR, 2017.

8. A draft sanction order is placed at Flag 'C' for approval.

Submitted for consideration and orders on para 7 above, please.


(Signature)
K. S. Narayanan
Under Secretary to the Government of India
Intercom: 2433
25.09.2026

Deputy Secretary (Admn)

```

## Document Referencing and Flagging Standards

Accurate referencing enables reviewing officers to trace every assertion back to its primary documentary source.

* **Correspondence Reference:** Cite the flag identifier alongside the correspondence page number (e.g., `PUC at Flag 'A', pp. 5–8/c` or `FR-I at Flag 'B', p. 14/c`).
* **Internal Note Reference:** Point to the exact note number and note-sheet page (e.g., `Note No. 8, p. 3/n`).
* **External File References:** Cite the full file number, volume, and note paragraph when citing precedents (e.g., `File No. 12015/02/2024-Admn, Note No. 14, dated 04.11.2024`).
* **Legal and Statutory Provisions:** Name the specific statute, rule number, and sub-clause (e.g., `Rule 56(j) of Fundamental Rules` or `Rule 194 of GFR, 2017`).

## Editorial Discipline: Administrative Tone and Phrasing

The Central Secretariat requires objective, impersonal, and temperate phrasing. A civil service note should not contain personal sentiment, editorializing, or aggressive prose.

| Prohibited Style | CSMOP-Compliant Style |
| --- | --- |
| "I strongly believe this proposal is great and we should do it immediately." | "The proposal appears administratively justified and is recommended for approval." |
| "The regional director made a terrible mistake in managing his inventory." | "The administrative delay resulted in the lapse of the previous quotation window." |
| "In my personal opinion, the rules are being applied unfairly." | "The extant rules do not contain provisions for relaxation in such circumstances." |
| "Submitted for necessary action." | "Submitted for consideration and orders on para 7 above, please." |

Officers should use the third person ("It is submitted that", "The proposal may be approved") instead of first-person declarations ("I propose", "We must decide").

## Critical Errors to Eliminate

Administrative proposals frequently stall in the Secretariat due to avoidable drafting defects:

* **Verbatim Duplication:** Copying the incoming letter onto the note sheet adds no analytical value. The note should capture the core issue, assess compliance, and state options.
* **Inaccurate Rule Quotations:** Citing rules from unverified memory leads to flawed sanctions. Every rule must be checked against current amendments and Department of Expenditure Office Memoranda.
* **Ambiguous Submission Endings:** Concluding with "Submitted for orders" without a concrete proposal forces the reviewing officer to reconstruct the file.
* **Concealing Financial Implications:** Omitting future maintenance costs, price escalation terms, or tax components creates unauthorized commitments and audit findings.
* **Erasure and Alteration:** Erasing, overwriting, or whiting out earlier notes is prohibited. If an officer disagrees with a subordinate note, they record a distinct, reasoned note directly beneath it.

---

## Final Submission Verification Checklist

Before a file moves to the next administrative level, confirm that:

* The file subject accurately describes the proposal.
* The Paper Under Consideration (PUC) and fresh receipts are properly tagged with physical or digital flags.
* Paragraphs run in unbroken numerical sequence with one main concept per paragraph.
* The applicable GFR, FR&SR, and departmental manuals are cited by specific rule and clause.
* Full financial implications, including one-time and recurring costs, are disclosed alongside budget availability.
* Concurrence from the Internal Finance Division (IFD) or the Legal Division is confirmed on the file record.
* The recommendation clearly states what is to be approved, by which authority, and under what conditions.
* The closing submission sentence points to the exact paragraph where the order is required.
* The note carries the full name, official designation, contact intercom, and date of the drafting official.
