Bridging the Gap: Extending a Static IP Office Network with a TP-Link C54

August 4, 2026 · 2 min · Phuchung Bhutia

Setting up network hardware usually feels like a plug-and-play affair. You unbox a router, plug in the WAN cable, and let DHCP handle the rest. It just works. But things get complicated when you are dealing with a static IP office environment. Honestly, bridging a fixed local area connection to a new Wi-Fi ecosystem requires a bit of manual maneuvering.

So, let’s walk through replacing an aging router and extending its reach using a TP-Link C54, keeping all the original credentials intact.

The first hurdle is getting the primary router to talk to an internet gateway that doesn’t hand out IP addresses automatically. If your network mandates specific parameters—say, a WAN IP of 10.182.12.163 with a 255.255.255.192 subnet—you have to hardcode these into the new hardware. You simply connect the main office LAN cable to the router’s WAN port, log into the router’s admin panel (typically at 192.168.0.1), and swap the connection type from standard automatic assignment to Static IP. Punch in your assigned gateway, which in this scenario is 10.182.12.129, along with your primary and secondary DNS servers. To be fair, missing just one digit here leaves you offline. Accuracy matters.

Once the primary router is live, you probably want to keep your existing devices connected without typing in a new password on a dozen different machines. You just change the new router’s wireless settings to match the old ones. Naming the SSID “Revenue Sec( Finance)” and reusing the original password ensures laptops and phones migrate over instantly.

Now, covering a dead zone. This is where the TP-Link C54 comes in as a range extender. Instead of running a long ethernet cable across the floor, you can grab the C54 and plug it into a wall outlet near the primary router for the initial setup.

You connect to the C54’s default Wi-Fi, pull up its admin page, and switch its operation mode from the standard router setting over to Range Extender mode. The device will reboot. Once it wakes back up, a quick setup wizard prompts you to scan the area for existing networks. You just select the “Revenue Sec( Finance)” network, type in the password, and let the extender sync up.

The final step is purely physical. You unplug the C54 and move it halfway between the main router and the area suffering from weak signal. It catches the stable connection and rebroadcasts it further down the hall, completing the setup without touching a single command line interface.
